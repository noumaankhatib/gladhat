**For:** Michael
**Status:** Draft for your approval. Nothing on the live or preview site changes until you tick the boxes below.

## Why we're sending this

Your copy has a lovely, consistent voice. While reading the whole site end to end, though, we noticed that three ideas come back on almost every page, often in nearly the same words:

- **"Every project begins with a conversation, not a brief."**
- **"See / look at things differently."** (About 15 variations: "look at the problem differently", "a different way of looking at the business", "a different way of seeing", "seeing business a little differently"…)
- **"The thinking behind the work."**

The five case studies also share the same closing heading ("What this project taught me") and the same prompt ("Recognise something of your own business here?"), and a couple of lines are word-for-word the same on two pages.

Consistency is part of the brand, but at this density a visitor who reads three pages hears the same pitch three times, and it starts to feel like a slogan, not a story. A storytelling site works best when **each page has one job** and says one thing well.

So below, for each repeat, we've kept the original, said why it repeats, and suggested a fresh line in your voice. Where a line would need a fact we don't have, we've marked it **[client to supply]**. Every suggestion is optional. You know your own voice best.

## What we need from you

These are the things only you can supply. They'll make the biggest difference to how real the stories feel.

1. **A portrait of you.** One real photograph (it can still be art-directed or atmospheric) for About and Contact. At the moment there is only a silhouette.
2. **Two or three client quotes, with permission to publish.** Each with the person's name, role and company. We can't write these for you.
3. **One result line per case study.** Server Factory already has real numbers. First Light, Tonbo, enSights and Provengo don't yet. Even a sentence will do. **[client to supply]**
4. **Your approval (or edits) on the table above.** Tick *Keep original?* on anything you'd rather leave alone, or write your own version beside it.
5. **Three article topics for Thoughts.** The page currently lists six themes but no articles. Pick the three you'd most enjoy writing first. (Our guess from the theme list: the Positioning, Invisible Work and Curiosity ideas, but you know best.)
